## 1. Overview

The Religions section has been rebuilt as a multi-level experience that introduces visitors to the religious and national diversity of Kurdistan. From a single main page, users can navigate into seven top-level themes, each with its own dedicated content. Two of those themes (Nations and Faiths) are themselves overview hubs that lead to individual detail pages for each people and each faith.

All pages are fully trilingual (English, Kurdish, Arabic) with automatic left-to-right / right-to-left layout, a consistent visual identity, and smooth entrance animations.

### 3.3 Nations (overview + 4 detail pages)

The Nations overview page presents four clickable cards. Clicking any card opens its own full detail page.

#### Kurds
- Language — Sorani and Kurmanji as the two main dialects, both official in the Kurdistan Region.
- Newroz — The Kurdish New Year (March 21), ancient Zoroastrian roots.
- Culture — Traditional dress, the Halparke dance, poetry, hospitality, and Sufi heritage.
- Cities — Erbil, Duhok, and Sulaymaniyah as cultural hearts of the Region.

*Tagline: Rooted in heritage. Building the future.*

#### Turkmens
- 5 Parliament Seats — Reserved seats under the 2005 amendment.
- Official Language — Recognized under Official Languages Law No. 6 of 2014.
- Culture & Arts — General Directorate for Turkmen Culture and Arts; Turkmen Language and Culture Day (November 17).
- Education — Mother-tongue education through the General Directorate for Turkmen Education.

*Tagline: Different roots. One homeland.*

#### Chaldo-Assyrians
- Syriac Language — One of the world's oldest living languages, officially recognized by the KRG.
- Ankawa — Erbil's main Christian district, home to the Chaldean Archdiocese and Babel College.
- Patriarch Returns — Assyrian Church of the East returned its patriarchal seat to Erbil in 2015; complex inaugurated in 2022.
- 5 Parliament Seats — The largest minority allocation in the Kurdistan Parliament.

*Tagline: The oldest nationality of the land, still here.*

#### Armenians
- History — Arrived in Kurdistan following the 1915 genocide and found a permanent home.
- Churches — Armenian Diocese oversees 14 churches, 5 in the Kurdistan Region, including Holy Cross Church in Erbil (2019).
- Community — Approximately 3,500–4,000 Armenians; Armenian Cultural and Social Association active in Erbil.
- 1 Parliament Seat — Reserved seat in the Kurdistan Parliament.

*Tagline: A community of memory, resilience, and belonging.*

### 3.4 Faiths (overview + 7 detail pages)

The Faiths overview presents eight clickable faith cards. Seven of them open their own full detail page (Yarsanism is the only one still pending content).

#### Islam
- Shafi'i School — The school of thought most Kurds follow, embraced since the 7th century CE.
- Sufism — 189 registered Sufi lodges; Qadiriyya and Naqshbandiyya orders shaped Kurdish society and culture.
- Ministry — Ministry of Endowments and Religious Affairs oversees 6,103 mosques and serves all religions.
- Ramadan & Eid — Shared seasons of fasting, prayer, and inter-faith solidarity.

*Tagline: Faith lived every day.*

#### Christianity
- Ancient Presence — Reached Erbil (Adiabene) in the 1st century CE through Apostles Addai and Mari.
- Churches — Four families (Catholic, Orthodox, Oriental, Evangelical) with active churches and monasteries.
- Easter & Christmas — Official public holidays in the Kurdistan Region.
- Pope Francis — Visited Erbil on March 7, 2021.

*Tagline: A living faith in Kurdistan.*

#### Yazidism
- Lalish — The sacred heart of Yazidism, in Shekhan district.
- Peacock Angel — Tawus Melek, central to Yazidi belief.
- Jemai — The major annual sacred gathering (October 6–13).
- Resilience — ~750,000–800,000 Yazidis in Kurdistan Iraq; August 3 declared Yazidi Genocide Day by the Kurdistan Parliament in 2019.

*Tagline: Rooted in faith. Living with dignity.*

#### Zoroastrianism
- History — The dominant religion before Islam; first fire temple in Iraqi Kurdistan opened in Sulaymaniyah in 2016.
- Three Pillars — Good Thoughts, Good Words, Good Deeds.
- Environment — Fire, air, water, earth treated as sacred.
- Nowruz — The most prominent festival, celebrated March 21.

*Tagline: An ancient flame of wisdom.*

#### Judaism
- Kurdish Jews — Ancient Eastern community of northern Mesopotamia; most chose to remain after the Babylonian exile.
- Torah — The Pentateuch, Talmud, Tanakh, Midrash, and Mishnah.
- Nahum Shrine — The Shrine of the Prophet Nahum in Alqosh.
- Heritage — Over 2,500 years of Jewish history in Kurdistan.

*Tagline: Rooted in memory and respect.*

#### Baha'i Faith
- Bahá'u'lláh in Kurdistan — Sargalu and Sulaymaniyah residence; "The Four Valleys" and "The Seven Valleys" letters to Kurdish Sufi leaders.
- Core Principles — Oneness of God, unity of humanity, gender equality, harmony of religion and science, justice.
- Free in Kurdistan — Represented in the Ministry of Endowments and Religious Affairs since 2015.
- Festival of Ridván — The most important Baha'i holiday (April 20 – May 2).

*Tagline: One humanity. One future.*

#### Sabean-Mandaeanism
- Meaning — Sabians (those who immerse in water) and Mandaeans (those of knowledge).
- Five Pillars — Monotheism, Baptism, Prayer, Fasting, Almsgiving.
- Mandaic Language — An ancient Aramaic dialect used in all religious ceremonies.
- In Kurdistan — Mandaean Cultural Association in Erbil; rights protected under Law No. 5 of 2015 and the Iraqi Constitution 2005.

*Tagline: Faith flowing through time.*

### 3.5 Shared Life

A dedicated page covering two groups of content:

**Group 1 — Shared Celebrations**
- Eid Al-Fitr & Eid Al-Adha — Official holidays celebrated with communal prayers, feasts, and visits; iftar tables prepared by Christians in solidarity with Muslims.
- Christmas & Easter — Official public holidays; egg coloring and Easter bread (ka'k).
- Yazidi Festivals — Red Wednesday (April), Jemai (October), Summer Forty Days (August), Day of Êzî (December) — all official holidays.
- Kakai Festival — Qewltas Festival in December.

*Tagline: Different traditions. One shared joy.*

**Group 2 — Heritage & Sacred Sites**
- Mosques — 6,103 mosques supervised by the Ministry of Endowments; Erbil Citadel Mosque, Grand Mosque of Akre, Jalil Khayat Mosque.
- Churches & Monasteries — Mar Mattai, Hormizd, Rabban Boya, and dozens of active churches.
- Lalish Temple — The sacred heart of Yazidism; Kaniya Spi and Zamzam springs.
- Zoroastrian Temples — Qazkapan in Dukan, Charsten Cave in Duhok; pre-Islamic Kurdistan still standing.

*Tagline: Every sacred place tells a story.*

# Discover Kurdistan Section

## 1. Overview

**Discover Kurdistan** is the high-level gateway into the broader Kurdistan story. It introduces visitors to the land, its people, its system of government, and its journey through time, and it links forward into the rest of the experience.

## 2. Main Page — `DiscoverKurdistan.tsx`

The page is built around a calm, earthy design with a soft cream background and a subtle Kurdistan landscape behind the content. It presents **four primary section cards**, each with an icon, title, and short descriptive line:

| Card | Subtitle |
|---|---|
| **The People** | Identity, culture, and resilience |
| **The Journey** | From 1991 to the present |
| **The System** | Parliament, government, and leadership |
| **The Land and Future** | Geography, symbols, protection, and progress |

Each card uses a gold-framed circular icon (people, map, landmark, mountain) and an accompanying illustration, giving the section a refined, museum-style feel.

## 3. Full Page Architecture

Discover Kurdistan is not just a single gateway — it opens into a **fully built, multi-level journey** with its own sub-overviews and individual detail pages.

### 3.1 The People
Overview page with three pillar cards, each opening its own detail page:
- **Who Are the Kurds?** — An ancient people of the Middle East known for courage, hospitality, and cultural richness.
- **A Shared Identity** — A people connected by language, history, tradition, and collective memory.
- **Story of Resilience** — The Kurdish journey of endurance, sacrifice, and rebirth.

### 3.2 The Journey
A timeline overview with five milestone pages tracing modern Kurdistan from 1991 to today:
- **1991** — Uprising and safe haven
- **1992** — First elections and the Kurdistan Parliament
- **Building Institutions** — Establishing the foundations of government
- **2005** — Constitutional recognition
- **Today** — Modern Kurdistan and ongoing development

### 3.3 The System
Overview of the Kurdistan Regional Government with four institution pages:
- **Parliament** — The legislative body of the Kurdistan Region
- **Government** — The Council of Ministers and executive structure
- **Presidency** — The office and role of the President
- **Prime Minister** — The office and role of the Prime Minister

### 3.4 The Land and Future
Overview connecting geography, identity, and progress, with four detail pages:
- **The Land** — Geography, mountains, rivers, and natural landscape
- **Peshmerga** — Kurdistan's defenders and protectors
- **Progress** — Development, modernization, and the future
- **Identity & Symbols** (Kurdish Language & Dialects) — Flag, language, and cultural symbols

## 4. Trilingual Support

Like the rest of the experience, every page reads its content from translation files (`en.json`, `ku.json`, `ar.json`), so all titles and descriptions automatically adapt to:

- English
- Kurdish (Sorani) — with right-to-left layout
- Arabic — with right-to-left layout

Safe English fallbacks are kept in each page so the experience always renders correctly, even if a translation entry is missing.

## 5. Animations

GSAP-powered intro choreography is applied across every page:

1. Decorative top line fades down.
2. Title and subtitle fade up.
3. Supporting text follows.
4. Section cards rise into view together at the end.

This creates a deliberate, cinematic opening rather than a flat page load — and the same rhythm is used on each sub-page so the whole journey feels unified.
